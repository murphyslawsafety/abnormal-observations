## Frozen source architecture

ROX-0012A calibrated a known quantum measurement-backaction architecture in which measurement strength/frequency changes subsequent state evolution and a measurement-strength regime crossover is present.

ROX-0013 froze the biological prediction before qualifying-source search:

A live biological measurement/readout procedure, when applied strongly or frequently enough, will alter the biological state/dynamics being measured relative to lower-dose/lower-frequency or unmeasured controls.

The portable object is measurement-as-transformation, not a common microscopic mechanism.

## Qualifying system 1 — mammalian live-cell fluorescence imaging

Source:
Del Rosario et al., Nature Communications (2025), PhotoFiTT.

Direct result:
- live adherent mammalian cells were exposed to controlled imaging-light doses;
- near-UV exposure caused dose-dependent mitotic delay and cell-cycle effects;
- significant delay was detectable at 0.6 J/cm^2;
- higher doses reduced mitotic events, increased arrest, delayed daughter-cell appearance, and reduced cellular activity;
- fluorescence-excitation experiments with MitoTracker showed imaging-associated delays relative to control conditions.

QUALIFIES: YES.

## Qualifying system 2 — yeast long-term fluorescence imaging

Source:
Schmidt et al. (2020), Preventing Photomorbidity in Long-Term Multi-color Fluorescence Imaging of Saccharomyces cerevisiae and S. pombe.

Direct result:
- widefield fluorescence excitation used for live-cell measurement changed cellular growth rate;
- the effect followed cumulative light dose;
- a no-observed-effect-level (NOEL) threshold was measured below which no photomorbidity was detectable;
- dose-response curves and ED50 values were measured across wavelengths and in two yeast species.

QUALIFIES: YES.

## Qualifying system 3 — zebrafish developmental live imaging

Source:
Scherz et al., Comparing phototoxicity during development of a zebrafish craniofacial bone using confocal and light-sheet fluorescence microscopy.

Direct result:
- live zebrafish skeletal morphogenesis was followed by fluorescence microscopy;
- frequent spinning-disk confocal imaging produced abnormal growth/morphological trajectories;
- lower-light light-sheet imaging permitted normal morphogenesis under the compared acquisition design;
- the effect was biological trajectory distortion, not merely lower image quality.

QUALIFIES: YES.
