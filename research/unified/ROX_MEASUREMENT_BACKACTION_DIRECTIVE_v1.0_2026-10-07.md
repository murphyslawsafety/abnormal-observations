# ROX MEASUREMENT-BACKACTION DIRECTIVE v1.0

**Date:** 2026-10-07
**Scope:** All ROX / Recursive Observability bridge experiments and, where applicable, Reality-Code / Unified-Code / Observer-Code experiments.
**Status:** CROSS-PROJECT METHODOLOGICAL AMENDMENT — NOT AN EUREKA

## 1. Core correction

Do not model observation only as a passive map:

`q:S -> Z`.

Where measurement can physically perturb the system, use an **instrument**:

`I_a:S -> (Z,S')`

where:
- `a` = measurement setting / strength / timing / basis;
- `Z` = recorded outcome;
- `S'` = post-measurement state.

The scientific problem therefore has two separable failure modes:

1. **representation loss** — q collapses distinct hidden states into the same observable;
2. **measurement back-action** — obtaining the observable changes the state being inferred.

EUREKA-005 already establishes the first problem cross-domain.
This directive adds the second as a mandatory falsification consideration where physically plausible.

## 2. Observer-backaction question

For every applicable experiment ask:

> Would the inferred transformation grammar be different if the system were measured less, measured differently, or measured after rather than during the evolution?

If yes, the experiment may be recovering the **measurement-conditioned grammar** rather than the unmeasured/native grammar.

## 3. Required state notation

Where back-action is plausible distinguish:

- `S_t^-` = state immediately before measurement;
- `Z_t` = observed record;
- `S_t^+` = state immediately after measurement;
- `I_{a_t}` = measurement instrument;
- `T_t` = subsequent system transformation.

Then:

`(Z_t,S_t^+) ~ I_{a_t}(S_t^-)`

and

`S_{t+1}^- ~ T_t(S_t^+)`.

Never silently identify `S_t^-` and `S_t^+`.

## 4. Back-action controls

Where feasible, preregister at least one:

- measurement-frequency randomization;
- measurement-strength randomization;
- weak/minimally invasive measurement arm;
- delayed measurement arm;
- destructive vs nondestructive readout comparison;
- sham interaction with matched energy/exposure but no information extraction;
- unmeasured or independently reconstructed trajectory control.

A positive back-action claim requires prospective divergence between these arms after matched controls.

## 5. Quantum boundary

Quantum measurement back-action is established physics, but it does **not** imply:
- human consciousness is required;
- retrospective data analysis changes a past experiment;
- every macroscopic measurement materially alters the target;
- Powerball outcomes change because an analyst looks at the data.

The imported lesson is methodological:
**observation can be a transformation, not merely a readout.**

## 6. Unified-Code relation

The observer map is upgraded from:

`q o T = Phi_T o q`

to an instrument-conditioned relation where applicable:

`I_a o T` versus `T o I_a`.

Order may itself matter:

`I_a o T != T o I_a`.

This creates a direct bridge to:
- EUREKA-004: noncommutative transformation order;
- EUREKA-005: observable equivalence need not imply hidden-state equivalence;
- EUREKA-006: a measurement grammar may be valid only within an admissible regime of measurement strength/frequency.

## 7. ROH implication

A stronger Recursive Observability architecture may require three maps, not one:

`state -> measurement interaction -> record + changed state -> future transformation`.

The record and the disturbance are separate outputs of observation.

Any claim that "observation observes itself" must test both informational and dynamical consequences when the domain permits them.

## 8. Claim boundary

This directive does not establish a universal observer effect.
It creates a cross-project falsification rule designed to prevent us from missing structure because the measurement process itself modifies that structure.

Canonical EUREKA tally at adoption: 6.
